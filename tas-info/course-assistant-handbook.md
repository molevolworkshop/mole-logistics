# MOLE course assistant handbook

## Introduction

This document attempts to summarize your duties as a course assistant for MOLE (short for the Workshop on Molecular Evolution) or STAMPS (Strategies and Techniques for Analyzing Microbial Population Structures), as well as describe how you’d go about carrying these duties out.

This manual was originally written by MOLE Course Assistant Marcus Fedarko in 2018. It has been revised since based on the experiences of later Course Assistants Alyssa McGurer (2022), Elena Korte (2023), Caitlin Randall (2025), and Anne Smith (2026).

In general, your duties as a course assistant include 1) setting up refreshments for breaks, 2) cleaning the equipment for that in between uses, and 3) attending to miscellaneous paperwork/logistical issues that come up during the course.

Note: Some courses might want you to be responsible for collecting money for the course T-shirts; for example, I did that for STAMPS 2018, but the TAs handled that for MOLE 2018. If you end up with this responsibility, just make sure to keep detailed records of who’s paid you. Evie Gomes, who works in the Bay Paul Center on the third floor of the Lillie Building, is a really good person to talk to for advice about this (as well as your other duties in this job). 

This guide will cover the first two of these duties; the third of these, at least from my experience, isn’t as involved.

### Disclaimers

Since this guide only covers the CA job for MOLE and STAMPS, it might not be that comprehensive for courses with an actual laboratory component (where the work being done isn’t entirely computational).
Another thing: I wrote this guide during the summer of 2018. So some of the people or places mentioned here might not be around if you’re reading this, like, ten years from now or something.

## Recommended things to do before the course

It’s a good idea to **get to Woods Hole a day or two in advance of when the course starts**, if you can. This’ll give you time to get familiar with the area, as well as with where the equipment you’ll need for breaks is.

There’s a map of the MBL available at https://www.mbl.edu/about/campus-facilities-resources/campus-maps. If you have a smartphone, I’d recommend **saving this map to your device** (or at least bookmarking it), so that you have a guide for where everything is. You’ll probably pick up pretty quickly on the building locations, but having a map like this will still be useful in the early days of the course (or if you have to stop by an office in some random building).

If you’re planning on bringing a car and running errands for the course, then you should look into **getting a parking pass** which will allow you to park on campus (and not in the Devil’s Lane parking area, which is inconvenient to get to after the shuttle stops running at night). Try to start contacting people about this at least a few days in advance of when you’ll arrive, to give the MBL time to argue with itself about whether or not to issue you a temporary on-campus parking pass. Bar Neck parking lot is a pretty convenient place to park as it is not too far from Loeb, but this is dependent on where the workshop is being held. Note: Cheryl Gherst, who works in the Homestead Building, is another super helpful person to talk to about getting parking set up, as well as about your other duties in this job (e.g. petty cash, paychecks). 

I’d recommend **asking your course directors if they have any place to store things overnight** in the building where the morning session of the course is taking place. During MOLE 2018, I stored most of the equipment in the Lillie building overnight, which forced me to spend a lot of time moving things between the Lillie, Rowe, and Loeb buildings. During STAMPS 2018, I was able to just store things in the Loeb building overnight and throughout the course, which made the process of setting up breaks so much easier. Note: It helps that the Loeb building has a break room with a large sink, as described in the Appendix. That makes cleaning out equipment a ton easier.

One more thing: you might want to talk to your course directors and/or Evie Gomes about **ensuring there’ll be tables set up** outside of the classrooms used during the course (per section Break Locations).

## Breaks

Probably your main duty as a CA is setting things up for breaks. People really like coffee.

### Break times

In the 2018 versions of MOLE and STAMPS, most days consisted of three course sessions: a morning session from 9am to 12pm, an afternoon session from 2pm to 5pm, and an evening session from 7pm to somewhere around 8pm–10pm. Each of these sessions has a short break (which usually takes anywhere from 10–15 minutes).

Each session’s break generally occurs about an hour and fifteen minutes to thirty minutes after the start of the session (so there are breaks at 10:15am, 3:15pm, and 8:15pm). 

Of course, many times the break will actually occur later than scheduled (sometimes up to 20–30 minutes late) or early (15 minutes early). In general, though, you should try to have things ready for each break by an hour after the start time specified above or 15 minutes before the break starts. 

### Session/Break locations

The MOLE 2018 morning sessions were in the Speck Auditorium (in the Rowe Building) and the afternoon and evening sessions were in Room G70 in the Loeb Laboratory. For STAMPS 2018, all of the sessions occurred in Loeb G70.

You should be able to set things up for a session’s break in the hallway just outside of that session’s classroom. These hallways should have a table set up where you can put various supplies for breaks (e.g. coffee creamers, sugar, tea).

There should be tables in Rowe just outside of the Speck Auditorium. For classrooms in other locations, you should check beforehand to make sure there’ll be a table.

Working for a course with multiple classroom locations in different buildings on campus complicates the process of setting things up for breaks, because you have to move things between buildings during the day. If some of these buildings don’t have large, kitchen-like sinks, then things get even more complicated, since filling up the coffeemaker with water becomes a lot more tedious. So I’d recommend finding a convenient place where you can reliably store equipment overnight. 

### What to have set up for breaks

This is going to depend on what the directors of your courses want. Ideally, each break will have all of the following things available:

#### ``Daily'' things

You’ll generally have to prepare these things, at minimum, each day (i.e. brewing a pot of coffee, or filling up a cooler with fresh ice).


- Hot coffee
- Host water kettle (for tea, hot chocolate, etc.)
- Cooler with ice, bottles of water, and cans of sodas
    - As an alternative to providing bottled water, a large water canister (5-gallon jug purchased from Walmart 05/23/25) can be used and students can fill the water bottles they receive at the beginning of the course. As a special treat, a tea or lemonade mix can be added. 


#### ``Nonperishable'' things

You can usually just keep these sorts of things on the cart assigned to you, or on the break table.

1. Creamers
2. Sugar
3. Stirring sticks
4. Tea and hot chocolate packets (as available)
5. Various nonessential things that make people happy: lids, coasters, napkins, ...


Side note: if you can, a box of feminine products (maybe a variety pack of tampons?) placed in the bathroom is a nice gesture and supports those students who cannot get transportation to town to buy these essential items. In addition, it would be really wise to provide hand sanitizer on the table and purchase sanitizing wipes so that people can wipe their own area to help minimize sickness. I also purchased local honey and a variety of teas including herbal, black tea, lemon ginger, and green tea. 

#### Trash 

Throughout the day you will likely produce a lot of trash. The MBL Campus sorts between cardboard and the remaining trash. To account for this, I kept a cardboard box to collect the broken down boxes (you will have a lot from drinks and snacks) and put a black trash bag in a paper bag to collect the remainding. This all may not be necessary depending on the location you are setting up, but G70 didn't have trash bins close enough or big enough to support the group. You will likely have to change the bags and dispose of the cardboard once a day.

#### Optional: snacks

Some courses might want to have snacks available for breaks, and some might not. Check with your course directors to figure out what they want. To make the process easier and to provide a record of what was purchased, an online order can be placed for pickup. However, this must be scheduled well in advance to ensure the items are ready on time. 
Also, I have found that people enjoy variety, so if possible, switch up some of the options for some different items or funky flavors. 

1. Bags of chips (individual servings are best)
2. Granola Bars
    - The students also seem to really enjoy cereal bars
3. Fruits (e.g. clementines, bananas, apples)

The rest of this guide assumes your breaks will only feature drinks; however, your course’s directors might want you to get snacks for the breaks as well. If you need to go get snacks, there’s a Walmart in Falmouth where you can purchase things like bags of chips and granola bars cheaply. Note: You can purchase course materials with your allotted ``petty cash,'' which you can obtain from the Homestead Building. Talk to Cheryl Gherst for more information. Also, there’s a Stop \& Shop in Falmouth where you can purchase fruits. Note: I don’t remember if the Walmart had fruits, but I feel like Stop \& Shop is probably a safer bet for those. The Stop \& Shop also has lots of snacks available, but when I went they seemed a bit pricier than Walmart (so I’d suggest going to both stores, if you can). The Walmart doesn't have any milk (2026), so I found myself going to the Aldis near Kappy's to get fresh fruit and milk. This store is also cheaper than Walmart.

#### Limitations on what you have ready for breaks

For MOLE 2018, I tried to have each of the three “daily” things (coffee, hot water, and sodas/waters) available for all three of the daily breaks. This is the ideal, but I don’t think it’s particularly necessary. If your course directors are ok with it, I would say it’s fine to only prepare a subset of these things for each break.

For STAMPS 2018, for example, I only brought out the cooler with sodas/waters for the afternoon and evening breaks. I also only prepared coffee in the morning (and just left it out on the “keep warm” setting for the afternoon and evening sessions). This gave me more time to focus on my other duties in the course (like paperwork, helping with teaching, etc.), as well as time to sit in on the course.

## Getting things ready for breaks

First off: don’t worry! This might sound like a large amount of stuff to be responsible for, and it kind of is, but once you get into a routine it becomes a bit more manageable.

The next section will talk about how you’ll go about getting stuff ready for the breaks.

### Cart

You should be able to pick up a cart (to store equipment on) from Martha Peterson’s office when you pick up a coffee maker. In 2026, there were several accessible carts near the entrance of Loeb that I was able to use. This seemed to work well, but you should also do your best to make sure the carts are consistently returned to the right place. 

### Coffee

The course coffee urn was purchased by Mark Holder, who was the director of the workshop in 2019. It is WestBend L5613F large capacity coffeemaker. The user manual is still online [here](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0023/5988/5891/files/L5613F_Large_Capacity_Urns_ALL.pdf?17366112731874500191) even though the coffeemaker itself has been discontinued.

The coffee maker’s manual is probably your best source of information on using it. 

You’ll generally have to make coffee each day of the course. The coffee maker takes around one minute per cup (plus the time taken to start the brewing/percolating process, which I think is about 8 minutes max), so if you’re making 60 cups of coffee then you should budget 68 minutes for the coffee maker to run to completion. (So if you’re trying to have 60 cups of coffee ready for a 10:15am break, you should **start the coffee maker at about 9am!**).

#### How much coffee do I need to make/get

This depends a lot on the cohort of students in your class, and what sort of drinks they prefer. During MOLE, I usually made 60 cups of coffee for the morning session, 40 cups for the afternoon session, and 20 cups for the evening session. And I always had a fair amount of coffee left over at the end of most breaks that I had to end up just dumping in the sink. (In retrospect, I could’ve probably made something like 50/20/15 cups for the morning/afternoon/evening sessions and wasted much less coffee.)

C: Personally, I found that 60 cups for the morning break and 40 (which is the minimum number of cups that can be made in the large 100-cup percolator) for the afternoon break were sufficient for 45ish people with minimal waste. 

Additionally, I found it challenging to remove the used grinds from the percolator, due to the diameter of the trash can and the hot temperature and inherent messiness of coffee grinds. To make it a bit easier, I sure to turn off the coffee maker before it was time to empty so it wouldn't be quite so hot. Additionally, to try to contain the coffee grinds I would empty them into a large brown paper bag (just what they gave me at the grocery store) and put that into the trash, instead of just dumping the grinds into the trash and it making a mess. Also, I would caution against wearing light colors due to the risk of staining from the grinds and residual coffee.

You could probably even brew 60 cups for the morning session and then just leave it on the ``keep warm'' setting for the rest of the day—that’s what I did during STAMPS 2018, and people seemed fine with it. Sometimes there was still some coffee left over at the end of the day, even. I don’t think there's a significant demand for coffee after the morning session; people seem to overestimate how much coffee they drink when you ask them about this in the morning. As mentioned in section Limitations, I’d recommend this way of doing things if your course directors are ok with it.

In 2026, there were roughly 60-70 attendees (including faculty). I found that making 80 cups of coffee before the morning session lasted until the start of the second session (at 2pm) on the keep warm setting. Then, I made 60 more cups and kept them on the keep warm setting for the rest of the day. People tended to not drink any more coffee after the start of the third session, but I kept a couple cups in the percolator just in case.

You should use 2.5 cups of regular grind coffee for 40 cups. Do not use fine ground coffee (i.e. the grind used for espresso or drip coffeemakers) and don't use extra coarse grind. The coffee will fall through the cracks in the sieve if it is too finely ground (and perhaps will stop up the cracks, leading to a flood). If the coffee is too coarsely ground, you will end up with weak coffee, which is by far the lesser evil. In general, if the coffee package does not specify any particular grind, then it is probably regular grind. If you can find a source of good coffee in a store with a grinder, go with that. Otherwise, we can get by with something like Folgers Gourmet Supreme (and you can also get coffee in bulk from Sodexo in Swope Center). In 2026, the Folger's Classic Roast Grounds worked well and didn't fall through the grate much.  

If the coffee maker malfunctions and you need to get coffee catered (as described in section \ref{sec:malfunctions}), then these same general rules apply. In terms of boxes of coffee (e.g. ``Boxes of Joe'' from Dunkin’ Donuts, where 1 box ~ 10 cups of coffee), I’d recommend getting 5 boxes for the morning session, for example. But it’s up to you, and what you think is appropriate. Note: Also, getting coffee catered is relatively expensive, to the point where I’d say that it makes sense to just get 5–6 boxes of coffee in the morning and use those for the rest of the day.

#### Making coffee

The coffee maker’s manual is the best source of information for this, but here’s what I did: 

First, **make sure the coffee maker has been rinsed out since its last use**. It’s ok if there are some grounds left over, but the more you can get out of the machine the better. 

Next, **put the pump tube back into the coffee maker**. It’s the long thing with a spring. If this is your first time using the coffee maker, it might already be in there.

Next, **fill up the coffee maker with cold water**, depending on how much coffee you want to make. If you want n cups of coffee, use n cups of cold water. I find that it’s easiest to use a jug or some other large container to fill up the coffee maker; in a pinch, the hot water kettle (assuming it’s at around room temperature) works well for this. If the education office gave you a relatively normal-sized coffee maker (e.g. the Hamilton Beach BrewStation model), you can also use its coffee tank to fill up the main coffee maker with cold water.

Next, **rinse the grounds basket in cold water a bit**. Apparently this helps prevent grounds from going through it, or something like that.

Next, you can **add coffee grounds to the grounds basket**. See section How much coffee? for help on how much grounds to use relative to how much water you’re using.

As per [this user manual](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0023/5988/5891/files/L5613F_Large_Capacity_Urns_ALL.pdf?17366112731874500191) :
       For 100 cups of water (assuming a cup is equivalent to 8 oz) use 6 1/4 cups of Ground Coffee. If the volume of the cups you are using to measure the grounds is not an 8 fl oz cup, you will have to take this into account and perform a conversion. 
       For 80 cups of water, use 5 cups of ground coffee. 
       For 60 cups of water, use 4 cups of ground coffee. 
       For 40 cups of water, use 2 1/2 cups of ground coffee. 
       You can manipulate these measurements to adjust the strength of your coffee. 
    

At this point, it’s time to **put the grounds basket on the pump tube**. It should just slide on. If the grounds basket has its own lid, you can put that on now, also.

Finally, **put the lid on the coffee maker, make sure it’s located in the place where you want to serve the coffee from, and plug it in**. The coffee maker should start brewing as soon as you plug it in (although if you’re using a different model, then it might behave differently).

For the Proctor Silex model: the light on the front should change from red to green to indicate that the coffee has finished brewing and is ready to serve.

#### Cleaning the coffee maker

You’ll need a room with a large-ish sink to do this in (e.g. Lillie 338, or Loeb 156). See the Appendix for more information.

When you’re emptying unused coffee or water out of the coffee maker, I’d suggest using the spigot to empty the bulk of it into the sink, and then just lifting the machine to drain the remainders (being careful to not get the coffee maker’s plug wet, of course).

It’s a good idea to at least rinse out the coffee maker after every time you make coffee. I’d look at the manual to see if it has any advice, but what I generally do is just rinse out each inner part of the coffee maker, and rinse out the interior of the coffee maker body once or twice. You can set the parts of the coffee maker (pump tube, coffee basket, lid, etc.) out to dry on the cart you were assigned, on paper towels or something.

You may want to actually clean out the coffee maker more thoroughly (e.g. with soapy water) if you’ve been using it for a while; as mentioned, I’d really recommend consulting the manual first to see if it has any special instructions.

#### What to do if the coffee maker malfunctions 

On July 27 of the 2018 MOLE course, I noticed that the coffee maker we had was literally leaking water into its own electrical socket. So I had to stop using it, which was. . . probably for the better! We ended up getting a new coffee maker, which is the Proctor Silex model I mentioned above. As an aside: if the education office issues you a really old 60-cup Saeco coffee maker, then they kept around the leaky coffee maker I just mentioned and are trying to pawn a broken machine off on you. **Don’t use it: ask for another coffee maker.**

If something like this happens to you—where you don’t have access to a working coffee maker, and still need to make coffee—then you can still get coffee catered in bulk from some actual coffee shops. I’ve gone to the Dunkin’ Donuts on Main Street in Falmouth a few times for this, and that’s worked out well. You might need to try multiple Dunkin’ Donuts locations, since they can be prone to running out of boxes of coffee. (Make sure to call at least a few hours in advance to reserve the boxes of coffee! It takes a while to make them.)

## Cooler with sodas/waters

This is one of the easier things to prepare for a break. Since the coffee can take a while to brew, I like to take care of this after the coffee has already started brewing (since filling up the cooler can be a significant time sink).
The coolers were stored in 2018 in the Lillie Building, room 302, although they might have been moved since then. There are two coolers available to you, but honestly you only really need to use one of them per day. (You can use multiple coolers if you’d like, but it’ll be more work to empty them both at the end of the day.)

### Adding drinks to the cooler

First, go get a cooler from wherever it’s stored. Note: If it’s your first day using the cooler, it should hopefully be clean. I’ll talk about cleaning the cooler later on in this section, so if someone messed up and your cooler needs cleaning right now then you can skip down in this section. 
You’ll need to fill it up with cans of soda and bottles of water. The exact proportions of what drinks to put in here are up to you; I like to make sure to include a lot of water bottles, and then sort of vary the soda types (while including a lot of each type of Coke, since people generally like those).

Try to put a decent amount of drinks in the cooler. If possible, it’s nice to avoid having to refill it in the middle of the day (but if that’s needed, it isn’t the end of the world).

### Adding ice to the cooler

Once your cooler is filled up, you need to put ice in it. Note: Well, you don’t really “need” to do that. If you’re in a rush, it’s ok to go without ice for a break. 

You can get ice from the loading dock of the Swope Building, in the area run by Sodexo (the food company servicing Swope). Note: A couple times during summer 2018, I had MBL staff ask me why I was using Sodexo’s ice machine, when there were apparently other “shaved ice machines” on campus. I have no idea where these machines are or if they even exist—I was explicitly told to get ice from Sodexo when I started the CA job—but if you also find yourself getting lectured at by some random MBL people, then it might be worth looking into this. 

If you’re standing in the lobby of Swope facing the front desk, head to the door straight back to the left of the desk. Then take an immediate right, and you’ll be on the loading dock area. From there, head left to reach a big room. In the far corner of this room (forward and left if you’re standing at the entrance point to this room, facing towards the room) is an ice machine. You can use the purple scoop thing to put ice into your cooler. Note: If there isn’t a scoop, go talk to someone who works for Sodexo and see what’s up. Please don’t, like, scoop the ice from the machine with your hands or something, because that would be gross. Also one of the Sodexo workers stressed that you should {\bfseries definitely not} use glass containers to scoop things, because if the glass breaks then Sodexo will have to drain and clean out the machine, and they will be sad.

You might want to shake the cooler around a bit to distribute the ice throughout it. Once you’re satisfied, you can just bring the cooler over to the break setup area and leave it there.

In 2026, Sodexo required that we inform them every time we used the cooler. I was able to talk to Steven Allen (steven.allen@sodexo.com) and set up two times (10am and 4pm) that employees would be present to allow me to get ice. This later changed to emailing the company (at Dining@mbl.edu) the day before each day of the workshop to allow me to get ice. I would suggest emailing the company ahead of the first workshop day to define how and when you are going to get ice during the workshop. I would also suggest asking whoever you talk to to communicate with the rest of the staff so they know why you are there. 

What worked for me was collecting ice in the 5 gallon water jug at 10 am (which then remained cold the rest of the day), draining the two previous coolers (only some of the ice melts over night), moving the ice from the soft drink coller to the alcohol cooler, and then filling the alcohol cooler at 4 pm with enough ice to also top off the soft drinks cooler at this time. This gave me ice for the whole day and into the social hours. It goes without saying, keep the cooler ice (dirty) and water ice (drinkable) separate to prevent contamination.

### Draining the cooler

You can drain the cooler out on any reasonable grassy location around campus, using the plug located on its back. I like to empty out the leftover cans, etc. onto paper towels and leave them to dry overnight (if you don’t have a place to store stuff outside of a classroom, you should be able to store them in Lillie 338 or 302).
If you end up storing coolers, etc. in Lillie: I like to drain the coolers outside on the grassy area behind Lillie because there usually aren’t people around to watch me flail about trying pick up a cooler multiple times, but the world is your oyster or whatever so do what you want.

### Cleaning the cooler

If you get gross stuff in the cooler (e.g. dirt), it might be a good idea to clean it out. There’s instructions for this online, but in general just use some soap and water, and make sure to at least get the soap out before you finish.

## Hot Water Kettle (for tea/hot chocolate)

Both MOLE and STAMPS had a fair share of people who preferred tea (or hot chocolate) to coffee, sometimes rather vocally. So accommodating those people is a good idea.
Martha’s office has a hot water kettle you can use. Assuming it hasn’t been replaced by the time you’re reading this, it’s a Chef’sChoice cordless electric kettle, model number 688. Using the kettle should be a fairly self-explanatory process. You can also find its manual online. One thing I’d suggest is to monitor the water level gauge on the side of the kettle -- if you fill it up beyond the MAX level, then water can leak out, which is bad. So, uh, don’t
do that.

### Preparing lots of hot water

If you have a second coffee maker or other heat-resistant repository available, you can heat up some hot water in the kettle, store the hot water in the second coffee maker, and then heat up some more hot water (repeating the process to fill up the second coffee maker with hot water as necessary). This way, you can have a lot of hot water ready at once. This helps if a lot of people in the course really like tea or hot chocolate, since it alleviates the need for people to go refill the hot water kettle.

In 2026, there is not a second coffee maker, but there are three hot water kettles which allow for the bulk production of hot water. I found that only some hot water was wanted during the day, so the metal kettle was enough. However, around the night session, I used both the white kettle and the metal one, periodically filling both to keep up with demand. The metal kettle is great because it can be put on a keep warm mode that only resets in around an hour orr so. However, the white kettle does not keep warm, so you just have to periodically turn it on to keep it warm.

### Lillie Building

Room 338 (on the third floor) is where Evie Gomes works. She’s super helpful, and she has lots of advice on how to do this job. Floor 3 of Lillie in general will probably end up becoming your home base of sorts throughout this job— you should be able to store supplies in Lillie 302 overnight, and you may be returning here throughout the day to rinse things out and refill things. Lillie 338 has a large-ish sink, like Loeb 156, which is really useful for cleaning things out.
    
### Swope Center

You’re living and eating here, probably! Sodexo is also based here, so if you have any food/coffee/etc. related questions they’re probably who to go to (along with Martha Peterson, if you’re using her equipment). You can also order stuff from them (e.g. bulk coffee) and charge it to your course, although I’m not 100\% sure on how that works.
